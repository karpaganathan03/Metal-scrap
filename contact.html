Frequently Asked Questions

INDUSTRIAL SCRAP SELLER GUIDELINES

Key information regarding weighbridge procedures, contamination standards, container deployment, and payment methods.

How quickly can Ferromax deploy roll-off scrap containers to our manufacturing plant?
For contracted industrial facilities and continuous manufacturing generators, we provide standard same-day or guaranteed 24-hour container drop-off across our regional service zone. We maintain an inventory of over 250 heavy-duty bins ranging from 20-yard low-profile containers to 50-yard high-cube boxes and leak-proof CNC swarf hoppers.
How is scrap weight verified and recorded upon arrival?
Every incoming delivery pauses on our certified, state-calibrated 80-foot pitless computerized truck weighbridges. Automated license plate recognition and overhead cameras document the load. The system prints a time-stamped Gross Weight ticket upon arrival and a Tare Weight ticket immediately after tipping, ensuring 100% transparent net weight accounting.
What testing is conducted on unknown or mixed non-ferrous alloys?
We deploy handheld Thermo Scientific Niton XL5 Plus XRF analyzers directly at the scale deck or receiving pad. In seconds, this non-destructive spectral assay determines exact percentages of Nickel, Chrome, Copper, Cobalt, Titanium, and Molybdenum, ensuring you are paid the full benchmark value for high-spec superalloys.
What payment terms and settlement options do you offer?
For verified industrial accounts, we provide same-day Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT / ACH), company check upon scale ticket confirmation, or scheduled net-15 / net-30 commercial settlement terms for ongoing high-tonnage manufacturing contracts.
Do you accept hazardous or contaminated scrap materials?
All scrap entering our yards must drive through Ludlum 375P portal radiation monitors. We strictly prohibit closed pressure vessels (unless cut in half), asbestos-containing materials, radioactive sources, and un-drained fuel tanks. CNC turnings with cutting coolants are accepted in dedicated fluid-recovery bays.
